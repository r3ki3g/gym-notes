# BroSplit next-set suggestion: methods findings

Compiled 10/21/26 from web research. Scope: how to suggest weight and reps for the next set, for two lifters
doing mostly machine and dumbbell hypertrophy work at 8-16 reps. Labels used below:
**[E]** evidence-based (study or meta-analysis), **[E/J]** derived from evidence but the exact number is a judgement call,
**[J]** judgement or common coaching/app practice. All percentages are relative to the load being lifted unless stated.

---

## (a) Strongest, most actionable principles

1. **Double progression is the default model for rep-range hypertrophy work, and every app that documents its rules uses it.**
   Keep the load and add reps until the top of the range is hit on the required sets, then add the smallest increment and
   go back toward the bottom of the range ([Alpha Progression glossary](https://alphaprogression.com/en/glossary/double-progression),
   [Boostcamp Auto Progression](https://www.boostcamp.app/features/auto-progression), [Liftosaur `dp(5lb, 8, 12)`](https://www.liftosaur.com/doc/liftoscript),
   [Hevy progressive overload guide](https://www.hevyapp.com/progressive-overload/)). [J, universal practice]
2. **Adding reps is as good as adding load for hypertrophy.** In trained lifters over 8 weeks, rep progression at a fixed load and
   load progression at fixed reps gave very similar muscle growth ([Plotkin et al. 2022, PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/14142)), and growth is similar across a
   wide range of loads when sets are near failure ([Schoenfeld et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7927075/)). So when the next weight step is a big
   jump, suggesting "+1 rep" is not a compromise. [E]
3. **Locally, one rep is worth about 2.2% to 2.6% of the load in the 8-16 rep zone.** The Epley relation
   (load x (1 + reps/30)) implies a rep is worth 1/(30 + reps) of the load. This matches a meta-regression of 952 rep-to-failure tests
   ([Nuzzo et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933212/)): bench press goes from 14.1 reps at 70.0% 1RM to 8.8 at 80.0%
   (Epley predicts 8.6), and leg press from 19.0 to 13.1 (Epley predicts 12.9). Use it only to convert between nearby loads and rep counts.
   It is not a reason to show a "1RM". Prediction accuracy falls as reps rise ([Reynolds et al. 2006](https://www.unm.edu/~rrobergs/478RMStrengthPrediction.pdf):
   R2 0.993 from a 5RM vs 0.955 from a 20RM on chest press), and the conversion varies by exercise and load
   ([Marzagao 2026, 303,494 sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17495)). [E]
4. **Relative step size decides when to add load.** A 5 lb step on a 20 lb dumbbell is a 25.0% jump. A 2 kg step on 10 kg is 20.0%, while
   2 kg on a 100 kg bar is 2.0% ([Alpha Progression, available weights](https://alphaprogression.com/en/glossary/available-weights)). By principle 3, a
   25.0% jump turns a 12-rep set into about 4 reps (8 reps lost). So the rep count that triggers a weight increase should rise with the relative step
   ("expand the rep range", as in [MacroFactor Smart Progression](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/305-understanding-and-using-smart-progressions)). [E/J]
5. **Reps drop across sets at a fixed load, more after sets to failure and short rests.** Across 29 studies of 4 or more sets to failure, set 2 reached
   about 70.0% of set-1 reps, set 3 about 55.0%, and sets 4-6 about 45.0-50.0%. Longer rest reduced the drop, but the curve kept its shape
   ([Nuzzo 2024 secondary analysis](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/4232), [Wolf, SBS 06/18/25](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=61886)).
   With 3 minutes of rest, lifters held 10 reps on all 3 sets of a 10RM bench, and with 1 minute they did not
   (Kraemer 1997, summarised in [Rahimi 2005](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-04-361.xml-Fulltext)). On leg press at 15RM, 3-minute rest gave 30.0% more total reps than
   1 minute ([Jambassi Filho 2013](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-12-138.xml-Fulltext)). Stopping at 1-2 RIR causes less acute fatigue than failure while
   giving similar growth ([Refalo et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/)). [E]
6. **When the next set is predicted to fall below the rep range, drop the load. Otherwise let reps fall.** ([Wolf, SBS 06/18/25](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=61886)) [E/J]
7. **Starting lighter and ramping up is legitimate.** In trained men, a crescent pyramid (12/10/8 reps with rising load) gave the same growth and strength as
   constant-load sets and drop sets ([Angleri et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28130627/)). By Epley, a 12RM load is about 90.5% of an 8RM load.
   So "first working set about 90.0% of the top set" is a normal pattern, not something to correct. [E/J]
8. **One heavier warm-up beats several light ones.** A warm-up set at 80.0% of 10RM x 5 gave more total volume over 3 sets to failure than 40.0% x 15
   or 60.0% x 10. An optional light set first may add a little ([SBS, heavier warm-ups, 08/14/24](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/heavier-warm-ups/)). [E]
9. **Autoregulation is at least as good as fixed prescriptions, but the signal must be measured.** RPE-based loading matched or slightly beat %1RM loading
   ([Helms et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29628895/)). Pooled autoregulated load vs percentage-based showed no significant strength difference
   (SMD 0.21, [Hickmott et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8762534/)). APRE, which sets the next load from the reps done on the previous set,
   ranked first for 1RM gain in a 2025 network meta-analysis ([LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4094556)). [E]
10. **Self-rated effort is useful but biased by about 1 rep.** On average lifters under-predict reps to failure by 0.95 reps
    ([Halperin et al. 2022](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/67)). Ratings are more accurate near failure and on sets of 12 or fewer reps
    ([SBS RIR guide](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/reps-in-reserve)). Less experienced trainees under-predict by 4-5 reps
    ([Steele et al. 2017](https://peerj.com/articles/4105)). The 0-10 RIR-based RPE scale (RPE 10 = 0 RIR) is the standard
    ([Zourdos et al. 2016](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/efef3b25-6701-4fb5-bb82-55fcd2a26027/full)). A coarse 3-4 option effort tap
    is about as precise as people actually are. [E/J]
11. **Day-to-day noise in reps to failure is about 1 rep (CV about 5.0-9.0%).** At 70.0% 1RM the SEM is 1.1 reps, ICC 0.86
    ([Mitter et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9070879/)). A first-set shortfall of 2 or more reps (or 15.0% or more) is therefore
    likely a real off day, not noise. [E/J]
12. **Subjective readiness questionnaires predict poorly on their own.** Wellness items have "limited predictive capacity" against load measures
    ([ACU analysis, 14,109 observations](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8zvq1/analysing-the-predictive-capacity-and-dose-response-of-wellness-in-load-monitoring)).
    Coaches and apps lean on early-set performance instead: JuggernautAI positions you in the day's range from warm-up performance
    ([JTS help](https://help.jtsstrength.com/en/articles/2-all-about-rpe-and-rir)), and VBT coaches compare warm-up bar speed to normal
    ([GymAware](https://gymaware.com/autoregulation-in-strength-training/)). [E/J]
13. **Deloads mean lighter training, not time off, and are usually reactive to stalls.** Strength and physique athletes deload about every 5.6 +/- 2.3 weeks
    for about 6.4 days ([Rogerson et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10948666/)), mainly by cutting volume and effort
    ([Bell et al. 2023 Delphi](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/32417/)). A full week off gave no hypertrophy benefit and slightly less strength gain
    ([Coleman et al. 2024](https://peerj.com/articles/16777)). Common stall rules: after 3 failed sessions, cut 10.0%
    ([StrongLifts rule via Jefit](https://www.jefit.com/routines/527484/stronglifts-5x5)). On a plateau, cut 10.0% load and 50.0% of sets
    ([Dr. Muscle](https://dr-muscle.com/what-makes-dr-muscle-different/)). [E for deload form, J for the triggers]
14. **Slow tempo lowers reps at a given load, so tempo sets are not evidence of lost strength.** At 80.0% 1RM, a 4 s eccentric or a 4 s pause produced fewer reps than a 1 s
    eccentric ([Pryor et al. 2011](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4023402?lng=en)). [E]
15. **Explain each suggestion and keep it overridable.** Explanations support transparency, scrutability and trust, but those aims can conflict
    ([Tintarev & Masthoff 2012](https://aura.abdn.ac.uk/items/acc6b40c-f682-4cb5-a679-6b239673af26/full)). Pre-filled defaults are strongly sticky
    ([Johnson & Goldstein 2003](https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/d/do-defaults-save-lives)), so a pre-fill must be conservative. [E/J]

## (c) Recommended algorithm sketch for BroSplit

Everything is computed **per person x exercise x side-mode x unit**, using loads exactly as logged (per side if per side). Units are never converted.

### c0. Normalise each logged set (cleaning step)

| Signal | Effective full reps `rEff` | Assumed RIR | Use for progression? | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| support `none`, no tags | reps | 2 (unknown; trainees tend to stop short) | yes | [J], informed by [Steele 2017](https://peerj.com/articles/4105) |
| support `failure`, or half reps > 0 (partials come after failure) | reps | 0 | yes. The most informative set | [J] |
| tag `Struggled last reps` | reps | 0.5 | yes | [J] |
| support `light`, tag `Help on the way up` | reps - 1 | 0 | yes, on rEff only | [J] |
| support `supported`, tag `Last 2 supped` | reps - 2 | 0 | yes, on rEff only. Never triggers an increase | [J] |
| tags `Ultra slow reps`, `Slow eccentric` | reps | n/a | **no**. A tempo set has its own track | [E] [Pryor 2011](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4023402?lng=en) |
| tag `Wrist pain` or free text matching `pain\|hurt\|tweak` | reps | n/a | **no increase**. Excluded from stall counting | [J] (pain-monitoring logic, [Silbernagel model](https://physicaltherapyfirst.com/blog/continued-sports-activity-using-a-pain-monitoring-model-during-rehabilitation-in-patients-with-achilles-tendinopathy/)) |
| `Good form` | reps | 2 | yes. Neutral | [J] |
| warm-up flag | n/a | n/a | excluded from everything except the warm-up template | [J] |
| drop-set continuation | n/a | n/a | excluded. Has its own drop-ratio template | [J] |

Rest before set n is approximately `end(n) - end(n-1) - reps(n) x 3 s` (use 8 s per rep for ultra slow). [J]

Capacity of a set: `cap = load x (1 + (rEff + RIR) / 30)`, used only to compare nearby sets.
Expected reps at load L: `rExp(L) = 30 x (cap / L - 1) - RIR_target`, with `RIR_target = 2`. [E] (Epley local fit, principle 3)

### c1. Rep band and step-aware "add load" threshold

- Rep band per exercise from history: `rLo` = 25th percentile and `rHi` = 75th percentile of clean working-set reps over the last 4 sessions,
  clamped to 6-20. Default when history is thin: `rLo = 10, rHi = 14`. [J]
- Relative step `s = step / load` with steps: blocks 1 pin, kg 2.5, lb 5 (per-exercise override allowed).
  For pins, `s = 1 / pin` assumes equal plates and no base weight. [J]
- **Add-load threshold** `rUp = max(rHi, ceil((rLo + 30) x (1 + s) - 30))`: after the step, the expected reps still reach `rLo`. [E/J]

| Example (rLo 10, rHi 14) | s | rUp | Expected reps after the step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leg press 100 kg -> 102.5 kg | 2.5% | 14 | about 13 |
| Dumbbell 50 lb -> 55 lb | 10.0% | 14 | about 10 |
| Machine pin 7 -> 8 | 14.3% | 16 | about 10 |
| Dumbbell 20 lb -> 25 lb | 25.0% | 20 | about 10 |

### c2. Session-to-session target (computed before the exercise starts)

Take the most recent **qualifying** session for this exercise (not marked easy, tired-flagged, tempo-only or pain), and its top working load
`Ltop` with best clean set `rEff`:

1. If `rEff >= rUp(Ltop)` on the first clean set at `Ltop`, and support is not `supported` -> **target `T = Ltop + 1 step`**. [E] (double progression)
2. Else if `rEff >= rLo` -> `T = Ltop`, with a rep goal of last best + 1. [E] ([Plotkin 2022](https://peerj.com/articles/14142))
3. Else (below band): hold `T = Ltop`. If this is the 2nd qualifying session in a row below band, `T = Ltop - 1 step`. [J] (Boostcamp holds, then estimates a lighter load)
4. Modifiers: pain in the last session -> `T <= Ltop`. Pain in 2 sessions in a row -> `Ltop - 1 step`, plus a "consider a variation" note. [J]
   More than 10 days since the exercise was last done -> `T - 1 step`, or about 5.0-10.0%, whichever is smaller in effect. [J] (Dr. Muscle light session at 10+ days; Fitbod reduces after breaks)
5. Smooth capacity across sessions: `cap = 0.7 x last qualifying + 0.3 x previous qualifying`. [J]

### c3. First working set of the day (fixes the "starts too heavy" complaint)

- **Anchor on how this person started last time, not on where they ended.** `F = lastFirstWorkingLoad + Δ`, where Δ is the c2 decision
  (+1 step, 0 or -1 step), capped at `T`. [J] (Their own habit, supported by [Angleri 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28130627/))
- Learn a start ratio `ρ = median(firstWorkingLoad / topLoad)` over the last 3 sessions. When there is no "last first set" (new side-mode or unit),
  use `F = roundDown(T x ρ)`. Default `ρ = 0.9`, about one rep-range "rung" below the top set. [E/J] (12RM/8RM load ratio is about 90.5%)
- Rep target for F: `rExp(F)`, shown as a 2-rep window (e.g. "aim 12-14"), clipped to `[rLo, rUp]`. [J]
- Warm-up suggestion: only if this person usually logs warm-ups for this exercise, or it is the first exercise for that muscle in the session.
  One set at about 75.0-80.0% of F x 5 reps, with an optional 50.0% x 10 before it. Round down. [E] ([SBS warm-ups](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/heavier-warm-ups/))

### c4. Next set within a session

After a working set at load L with `rEff`, assumed RIR and planned rest:

1. **Predict reps at the same load.** [E/J] ([Nuzzo 2024](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/4232), [Rahimi 2005](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-04-361.xml-Fulltext))
   - Set ended at RIR 0 (failure, assisted, partials): `rNext = rEff x (1 - d)`, with `d = 30.0%` if rest is under 120 s,
     `20.0%` at 120-180 s, `15.0%` above 180 s.
   - Set ended at RIR 1 or more: `rNext = rEff - 1` if rest is under 90 s, else `rEff`.
2. **Decide the load, first matching rule wins:**
   - Tempo-tagged set -> same load, same tempo, `rNext`. [J]
   - Pain tag -> same load or -1 step, never up. The reason line names the pain note. [J]
   - `rEff >= rUp(L)` and no assistance -> **+1 step** (ramping style). Rep target = `rExp(L + step)` minus the fatigue from rule 1. [E/J] (APRE adjusts the next load from the previous set's reps: [Mann, APRE](https://training-conditioning.com/article/understanding-apre-part-1/))
   - `rNext < rLo` (or under 6) -> drop the number of steps whose predicted reps land closest to `rLo + 1`, at least 1 step. [E/J] ([Wolf, SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=61886))
   - Otherwise -> same load, target `rNext`.
3. **Drop-set continuation:** load = person's median historical drop ratio for this exercise. Default 0.75-0.8 x L, rounded down. Reps "to failure". [J]
4. Never suggest +2 steps unless `rEff >= rUp(L + step)` too. APRE's larger jumps for big rep surpluses support this. [E/J]

### c5. Tired / strong day detection (first working set of each exercise)

- Compute `shortfall = rExp_today(F) - rEff`, both normalised to the same RIR via c0. Skip this if the set is tempo-tagged, if rest was under 90 s,
  or if there are fewer than 2 qualifying past sessions.
- **Flat for this exercise** if `shortfall >= max(2, 15.0% of rExp)`. Then: no load increases for the rest of this exercise, this session is excluded from
  progression and stall counting, and the reason line reads "3 reps under your usual at pin 7, holding load". [E/J] (noise SEM about 1.1 reps: [Mitter 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9070879/))
- **Tired day** if 2 or more exercises in the session are flat, or the user tapped "Tired". Then the first-set suggestion for later exercises is -1 step
  (or about 5.0%), and the day is excluded from stall counting. [J]
- **Strong day** if the surplus is at least `max(2, 15.0%)` -> allow the c4 +1 step on the next set even if the person usually holds. [J] (JuggernautAI moves you within the range from warm-up performance)
- **Easy day toggle** (deliberately light) -> all first-set suggestions -10.0%, no increases, the session is ignored by c2 and c6. [J]

### c6. Stall and deload rules

- **Exercise stall:** 3 consecutive qualifying sessions where best capacity does not beat the previous best by 1 rep-equivalent (about 2.5%).
  Suggest -10.0% (rounded down to a step), then rebuild with c2. Alternatively offer a rep-band change (e.g. 12-20 instead of 8-12). [E/J] (StrongLifts/Liftosaur style, Dr. Muscle -10.0%)
- **Exercise regression:** best capacity down 5.0% or more across 2 qualifying sessions -> one lighter session (-10.0%, stop at about 3 RIR). [J]
- **Global deload prompt:** in the last 7 days, 50.0% or more of the exercises trained are stalled or regressing, or there were 2 tired days ->
  suggest a lighter week: about -10.0% load, -30.0% to -50.0% sets, stop at 3 or more RIR. **Do not suggest days off.** [E for form: [Bell 2023](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/32417/), [Coleman 2024](https://peerj.com/articles/16777); J for triggers]
  With 12 days of data, these rules should stay dormant until each exercise has about 4 or more sessions.

### c7. Rounding to units

- Round every computed load to the exercise's step grid in its logged unit. **Increases are always exactly 1 step.**
  For decreases, choose the step whose predicted reps land closest to the target, and on a tie choose the lighter one. [J] (Boostcamp rounds down)
- When the step is large relative to the load (`s >= 10.0%`), prefer a rep target over a load change. c1 already does this through `rUp`. [E/J]
- If a suggestion is below the lowest available load (pin 1, the lightest dumbbell), keep the minimum and raise the rep target. [J] (Fitbod's empty-bar complaint)

### c8. Default numbers summary

| Parameter | Default | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Rep worth | 1/(30 + reps) of load (2.6% at 8 reps, 2.2% at 16) | [E] Epley; matches Nuzzo 2024 |
| RIR assumed for unlabelled sets | 2 | [J] |
| Rep band when no history | 10-14 | [J] |
| Add-load threshold | `max(rHi, ceil((rLo+30)(1+s)-30))` | [E/J] |
| First-set ratio when no history | 0.9 of target top load | [E/J] |
| Rep drop after a failure set | 30.0% / 20.0% / 15.0% for rest <2 / 2-3 / >3 min | [E/J] Nuzzo 2024, Kraemer 1997 |
| Rep drop after a non-failure set | 1 rep if rest <90 s, else 0 | [J] |
| Off-day threshold | shortfall >= max(2 reps, 15.0%) | [E/J] Mitter 2022 |
| Stall | 3 qualifying sessions with no capacity gain over 2.5% | [J] |

## (d) Recommended UI

1. **Pre-fill the weight. Show reps as a target.** The weight is chosen before the set, so pre-fill it with the suggestion; that keeps one-tap logging.
   Reps are logged after the set and must reflect what happened, so show the rep target as a styled hint ("aim 12-14").
   Two options: leave the field empty with buttons around the target, or pre-fill it in a visibly "target" style until touched.
   Defaults are sticky ([Johnson & Goldstein 2003](https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/d/do-defaults-save-lives)), so a pre-filled
   rep count risks being logged unchanged. [E/J]
2. **One-line reason under the inputs**, always: e.g. "Start at pin 7: you opened at 7 last time and hit 15 (>= 14), so +1 rep".
   Or "Holding: wrist pain noted last session". Or "Tired day? First set 3 under usual". This follows MacroFactor's wand pattern
   ([help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/391-what-does-the-smart-progression-wand-mean)). [E/J]
3. **Three tap-to-fill chips:** `Suggested 7 x 13-15`, `Last start 7 x 14`, `Last top 8 x 10`. Tapping one fills the form,
   like Hevy's tap-on-previous ([help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/34105442929943)). [J]
4. **Easy override:** the ± buttons on weight move exactly one unit step (1 pin, 2.5 kg, 5 lb); reps ± 1. Once the user edits a field, never
   re-suggest into it. If someone overrides in the same direction 3 times for an exercise, update `ρ` or `rLo/rHi` for them. [J]
5. **Confidence cue:** "based on 1 session" vs "based on 4 sessions". With no history, show only last-time values and no "smart" claim. [J]
6. **Setting:** initial fill = "Smart" or "Last time", as in MacroFactor
   ([help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/305-understanding-and-using-smart-progressions)). [J]
7. **Session banner** when the tired, strong or stall rules fire, with a dismiss option ("No, I chose an easy day"). Dismissing marks the day as easy. [J]
8. **Suggest for the person whose set it is**, not for the phone owner. They log each other's sets. [J]

### Minimal extra input worth adding (ranked)

1. **"Easy day" toggle per session.** Cheapest fix for the stated pain point: it keeps deliberately light sessions out of progression and stall logic. [J]
2. **Optional one-tap effort per set: `Easy (3+ left) / Solid (1-2 left) / All-out (0)`**, with existing `failure` support as the 0 case.
   Three options match the measured precision of self-rated RIR (about ±1 rep, [Halperin 2022](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/67)).
   It turns the assumed RIR of 2 into data. Leave it optional, as Alpha and MacroFactor do, and infer RIR when it is blank. [E/J]
3. **Per-session feeling (tired / normal / fresh)** as a weak prior only. It counts as one "flat" flag in c5 and never moves loads by itself
   ([wellness evidence is weak](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8zvq1/analysing-the-predictive-capacity-and-dose-response-of-wellness-in-load-monitoring)).
   Session RPE is reliable, with ICC 0.88 ([Day et al. 2004](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/TREINO%20DE%20FORC%CC%A7A/Monitoring%20Exercise%20Intensity%20During%20Resistance%20Training%20Using%20the%20Session%20RPE%20Scale.pdf)),
   but it is retrospective and does not help the first set. [E/J]
4. **Per-machine plate weight (optional)**, so pin steps become real relative steps. [J]

## (e) Open questions

1. **Pin-to-load mapping.** Is `s = 1/pin` close enough? Do any stacks have a base weight or uneven plates? The same exercise on different machines will need separate tracks.
2. **Typical RIR of their unlabelled sets.** The data agent can check how often `failure` or partials appear, and how reps fall from set 1 to set 2 at the same load with known rest, to calibrate the assumed RIR of 2 and the drop percentages in c4.
3. **Their actual rest.** Because they alternate sets, rest is probably the partner's set plus logging (about 1.5-3 min). If it is that consistent, the rest adjustment in c4 matters little.
4. **Rep bands per exercise.** Should they be inferred (c1) or set by the user? Inference may lock in habits, such as always stopping at 12.
5. **The per-exercise constant k = 30.** With enough failure sets at 2 or more loads, fit k per exercise; the evidence says the conversion varies by exercise and load ([Marzagao 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17495)).
6. **Single-arm per-side reps and asymmetry.** Should progression follow the weaker side?
7. **Tempo track.** Should a tempo set's load progress on its own track, or always copy the last tempo load?
8. **Offline validation before shipping.** Replay the 411 logged sets and score how often the suggestion lands within 1 step and 2 reps of what they actually did. Report first sets of the day separately from later sets, and compare against the current "last set" pre-fill. A reasonable bar is beating the current pre-fill on first sets by a clear margin, e.g. 20.0 percentage points. [J]
9. **Evidence gaps.** No study tests rep drop-off for sets stopped at 1-3 RIR with about 2 minutes of rest, which is the users' likely regime. The c4 numbers there are judgement calls.
   App rules for Hevy Trainer, RP and Fitbod are not fully published, so the table relies on help pages and third-party summaries.
